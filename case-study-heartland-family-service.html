Case Study · Heartland Family Service

Improving a Marketing Request Process Without Buying New Software

Heartland Family Service's marketing team handles requests from staff across the agency, and the process around those requests had outgrown the way the team actually worked. Roles weren't always clear, it was hard to see where a project stood, and finished work could sit waiting. We clarified the stages and owners, improved the request form, and automated the handoff so new requests set themselves up.

The Challenge: A Process That Had Outgrown the Work

Staff across the agency request marketing pieces, and a small team turns those requests into flyers, postcards, social posts, and more. The foundation was sound, but the process had grown over time and no longer matched how the work really happened. Requests arrived inconsistently, status was tracked in more than one place, the owner of each step wasn't always clear, and finished work could stall at the end. The team needed clearer stages, defined sign-offs, and a request form that gave them what they needed up front.

When I walked through the team's tracking board card by card, the friction showed up in three places:

1 · What came in Requests arrived with four pages of copy for a one-page flyer, and photo choices were open-ended. One flyer carried 34 comments of back-and-forth.
2 · Status, three ways Priority lived in a label and a custom field that disagreed. Status was typed into card titles in capitals: "READY FOR REQUESTEE REVIEW," "FUNDING STATEMENT NEEDED."
3 · The stalled back half Cards marked complete months earlier were still waiting to be uploaded, and one proof was three months overdue. Nobody clearly owned the last steps.
The detail that changed the scope: grant-funded print pieces need a funding statement from Development before they can be approved. Marketing's timeline depended on another department, so the fix had to include both teams in the room.

The Approach: Process First, Automation Second

The marketing manager had already drafted a new flow, and it was a good one. My job was to pressure-test it against what the board showed, not to replace it with mine. I set three rules for the engagement.

1 · Evidence from the board Every recommendation pointed to something real on the cards: comment counts, overdue dates, duplicated fields.
2 · Fix the rules before the tools Copy limits, revision caps, and clear owners come first. Automating a broken process only makes it break faster.
3 · Use what they already have Microsoft 365 and Trello were already in place. No new platform, no premium license.

What We Built Together

1 · Defined the stages

An earlier 13-stage draft became nine: Request Received, Copy Queue, Copy In Progress, Copy Review & Proof, Design Queue, Design In Progress, Design Review & Proof, Fulfillment, and Done. Copy is locked before design starts, which removes the most expensive kind of rework.

2 · Named who signs off at each stage

Every stage has an owner by role, including Development's funding-statement check. Nothing sits in a column waiting for "someone."

3 · Redesigned the request form

Nearly every question became a click instead of a text box, so the answers are clean enough to automate. The form now caps copy, flags grant funding early, and settles the photo question up front.

4 · Connected the form to Trello

A form submission now creates a complete Trello card in Request Received: titled, labeled by category, dated, with every answer in the description. In testing, the first card appeared in one second with no one retyping anything.

5 · Set to-dos and timelines for every stage

The full team walked each stage's checklist together and agreed on realistic timeframes, so anyone can see where a project stands and what's due next.

6 · Documented it for handoff

A step-by-step SOP with annotated screenshots covers the form, the automation, and the Trello rules, so the system can be maintained without me.

Why It Matters

Most "we need a better tool" problems are really "we need clearer rules" problems. Settle the stages, the owners, and what a good request looks like first. Then the tools you already pay for can do most of the rest.
