A CRM Inside Gmail and Google Sheets, Built for a One-Person Nonprofit

The Jet Award Foundation's sponsor, donor, and grant information was scattered across payment platforms, checks, and emailed lists. Now it lives in one sheet, with a live dashboard that updates from the director's inbox every 15 minutes.

The Challenge: No Single Place Showed Who Had Given

Mike'l is the foundation's director, fundraiser, and communications team in one. Money and leads came in through Zeffy, Square, the auction and gala platforms, checks and emails that arrived in the mail, and lists passed along by his board president and others. Grant deadlines showed up as emails.

Nothing showed in one view who had given, who had been asked, or what was due next. Pulling it together for the quarterly board budget report meant doing it by hand.

The constraint that shaped everything: a one-person shop cannot absorb a new tool. A system that asked him to log in somewhere new, or retype what he had already written in an email, would likely end up unused.

The Approach: Build Around His Workflow, Not Over It

Before writing anything, I spent 90 minutes mapping how the foundation actually runs, step by step, using only the times and numbers Mike'l stated himself. Two things stood out. He works from Gmail, even keeping a draft email as his scratchpad for the weekly highlight post. And that weekly post takes about an hour and works fine, so I left it alone. That shaped three rules.

1 · Gmail is the interface He sends and labels email the way he already does. The CRM listens.
2 · Google Sheets is the home It is familiar, he can filter and export it himself, and nothing is locked inside a platform he has to learn.
3 · A person decides the money The CRM flags and suggests. It never invents a dollar amount.

What I Built: A Label in Gmail Becomes a Row and a Dashboard Number

Mike'l changes one habit: when someone says yes, he labels the email. Everything after that runs without him, except typing the amount.

1 · Mike'l labels the email

When someone says yes to a gift, he applies the Gmail label CRM/Committed.

2 · The CRM picks it up

Within 15 minutes it marks the person Committed, dated from that thread, and relabels the email CRM/Committed - Logged.

3 · The sheet flags what's missing

The Action Needed? column says ADD $ AMOUNT, and a banner shows on the dashboard. Hovering over the amount cell lists any dollar figures the CRM saw in the thread.

4 · Mike'l types the final amount

This is the only manual step. The CRM never fills in dollars on its own.

5 · The dashboard updates

The gift counts toward the season total and shows in the event and year views.

The label is the main path. The same Gmail connection handles the rest of his day:

What Keeps It Trustworthy

A CRM only helps if the director believes its numbers. A few rules protect that.

No guessed dollars

When a thread mentions an amount, hovering over the cell shows the figures it saw. Mike'l types the final number.

Status only moves up

Email activity can move a contact forward, never backward, and a gift from a prior year stays out of this year's total.

Unclear cases go to a review label

If the CRM cannot match a thread to a person, it files the thread under CRM/Needs a look instead of guessing.

Imports get a second look

A list from Zeffy, Square, or a gala goes into its own tab first. I merge it and check for duplicates.

Tested before it touched his inbox

I ran the label logic against a mock mailbox before it went near his real Gmail.

Documented

He has a one-page guide with a labeled picture of the dashboard, so every number has a known source.

Why It Matters

If your team already works in email and spreadsheets, you do not need a new platform to get a real CRM. Start with how the work actually happens, then build around it.
